Agent Handoff: Agentic Real-to-Sim for Robotic Manipulation
===========================================================

Core task:
"Reconstruct everything in the observed scene into a high-fidelity digital twin.
The simulated images should match the source images as closely as possible, and
the simulated robot interactions must predict new physical outcomes. You must
independently inspect, experiment, correct, render, compare, and repeat."

You are the implementing research Agent.

"Exactly the same" is the optimization target, not permission to invent a perfect
metric, hidden back surfaces or unknown physics. Measure both rendered-image
errors and predictive-contact errors. A correct mass with incorrect shape, handle,
colors or light is not a finished scene; a pretty render with incorrect contacts
is not a finished twin. Preserve negative evidence and report uncertainty rather
than faking a 100% score. Do not invent success, read held-out ground truth, or
silently relax metrics. Do not stop merely because a script ran, an image looks
good or a loss decreased.

Choose the best verified simulator(s) for your twin.
  - possible physics engines: PhysX (via Isaac Sim), MuJoCo/MJX, Newton
  - possible renderers: Blender/Cycles, Isaac Sim RTX

Reconstruct and compare objects, support, background, colors, materials, shadows
and illumination from the start; do not defer visual verification. Add
higher-fidelity renderers and richer lighting as image residuals show the need.
You may use separate engines for physics and rendering, provided both read the
same canonical scene and every rendered frame is driven by simulated state; the
evaluator must be able to run your full pipeline from your submission. You may need to ask for GPU access. If something you need is not installed,
ask. Choose the engines that give the most faithful recreation, not the easiest.


Project summary (orientation only)
----------------------------------
A real tabletop scene sits in front of a real robot arm that you reach only
through a robot and sensor API over the network. From calibrated RGB-D sensing, a
known robot model and the API, reconstruct the scene for your seed into a twin.
Ground truth (object dimensions and masses, and the outcomes of held-out robot
actions on the real scene) is measured by the operators and hidden.

A good twin:
  - renders images that match the camera (geometry, colors, materials, table,
    room, lights, shadows), including scene states you never saw;
  - predicts new physical outcomes: when robot actions you never executed are
    run on the real scene, your twin's objects move the way the real ones do.

Lighting and texture are as important as geometry. Lights: your twin must
contain the scene's lights: how many, the type of each (point, spot, area,
directional, ambient or sky), and each light's position or direction, colour
and intensity. They are judged through renders of scene states you never saw.
Model surfaces as textures where they show patterns, including their shine.
Never bake lighting into textures. Camera exposure and white balance are fixed;
do not fit them per image. Mark surfaces you never observed as unobserved.

You should try using algorithms to figure out what the physical and appearance
parameters are. Examples include (but are by no means limited to) system ID,
inverse rendering, real2sim, etc.

This is a real robot. Every motion passes a safety layer and runs immediately; a
human operator watches with the emergency stop. Sensors are noisy, objects are
not perfect primitives, and nothing can be undone: there are no resets (see
Episodes below).

Use the full wall time (4 h, API calls included). Submit early only if you can show that no remaining budget or time could reduce your largest errors. Unused time and unused budget earn nothing. Until 20 minutes before the deadline, keep working on whichever error is largest: image error by region, pose and force error on your own held-out actions, or the parameters still marked "assumed". Keep the packaging work (records, predictor, report) within the last 20 minutes.


Assumed values. Every value you mark "assumed" in params.json needs a reason in a new file, assumptions.json:
  - "probe_tried": the probe(s) you ran to identify it, and why they didn't pin it down;
  or
  - "not_identifiable": why no probe available within the API and the remaining budget could identify it, for example mass and friction showing up only as a product in pushes;
  or
  - "no_effect": evidence that the value doesn't change any outcome your twin predicts, for example a welded part's mass.
  An assumed value with none of these counts as unfinished work, not a finished guess.
  Before you submit, list each assumed value whose reason is "not_identifiable" and say which probe you would run if you had more budget.

Rendering rule
--------------
Everything in a twin render must be simulated: objects, robot, table, walls,
floor, background and lighting. Model the background as 3D geometry with fitted
colors and materials, lit by your fitted lights. Observed images may only be
used as comparison targets and for fitting parameters. Never use camera images,
crops of them, or anything derived pixel by pixel from them as a backdrop,
background plate, environment map, texture, mask fill or composite layer, and
never paste or blend observed pixels into a render.

No photo textures: every material must be procedural (shader nodes, built-in
textures, flat colors) or come from a standard asset library, with its
parameters tuned to match the images. Sampling colors or roughness values from
the images is allowed; textures cropped, projected, or generated from them
(including by image-conditioned generative models) are not. A render that
contains observed pixels does not count as a reconstruction, and any
image-match metric computed on it is invalid.


The API
----------
The server URL and your token are given separately. Every request carries the
header "Authorization: Bearer <token>". JSON requests; array responses come as
NumPy .npz files (load with allow_pickle=False) with a JSON string "meta". Units
are SI (metres, seconds, radians, newtons). World frame: z up, the table top is
the plane z = 0, origin directly below the robot base; x points away from the
robot towards the camera, y to the robot's left. The robot base sits on the
table at (0, 0, 0.0134).

  GET  /v1/info          description, your seed, budgets, cameras, primitives,
                         logging rate, table extent
  GET  /v1/budget        remaining budget
  POST /v1/reset         {"seed": "<your seed>"}  call once, to start; it sends
                         the robot to its home pose and waits for the operator
  GET  /v1/calibration   per camera: width, height, K (OpenCV convention),
                         T_parent_cam, parent frame; depth in metres along the
                         optical axis, 0 = invalid
  POST /v1/observe       {"camera": "zed"}  returns rgb, depth, t, tcp_meas,
                         yaw_meas
  POST /v1/execute       {"primitive": "...", "params": {...}}
                         returns t, tcp_cmd, tcp_meas, yaw_cmd, yaw_meas,
                         width_cmd, width_meas, wrench, frame_t, frames_rgb,
                         frames_depth; meta has status ("ok",
                         "protective_stop" or "rejected", with a reason) and
                         events (stop_force, protective_stop, target_not_reached)

The API calls your scene a "seed"; you have exactly one, listed by /v1/info.
Actions take tens of seconds and answer directly: use request timeouts of 10
minutes or more. Errors: 400 invalid call (costs nothing), 401 token, 403 not
your seed, 409 the operator must clear a robot fault (retry about once a
minute), 429 budget exhausted.

There is one camera, "zed": a fixed ZED 2i stereo RGB-D camera, 1280 x 720, in
front of the robot looking back at it. It never moves. Its depth is computed
from stereo, not measured directly. Check the calibration, and make sure there
is no bias in your perception before performing any actions.

Episodes: one episode, no resets. The scene as you first see it is the canonical
start state. Every action changes the real scene, and nobody puts it back: the
scene after an action is the scene your next action acts on. Re-observe after
every action.

Motion: you command the gripper through primitives; the controller moves the
full arm. A protective stop ends the action early, backs off 2 cm and is free;
you can continue after re-observing. "target_not_reached" means the arm
stopped short; always use tcp_meas, never the commanded target.

  move_to     {tcp: [x,y,z], yaw?, speed?}                         free move
  home        {}                                                   free move
  push        {contact_point: [x,y,z], direction: [dx,dy],
               distance, speed, yaw?}                              contact
  touch       {point: [x,y,z], direction: [dx,dy,dz], max_travel,
               speed, stop_force, yaw?}                            contact
  grasp_lift  {center: [x,y,z], yaw, open_width, lift, hold?}      contact

Each primitive rises to a safe travel height, moves above its start point, does
its work and returns to the travel height. A touch stops when the measured force
exceeds stop_force. Any action stops if the force exceeds the protective limit.

Budget: primitives are charged by type (/v1/info lists contact_primitives);
protective stops and rejected calls are not charged. /v1/info lists the
observation budget. Ranges are enforced by the server; an invalid call is
rejected with a message and costs nothing.

The robot is a Flexiv Rizon 4s 7-axis arm with a Flexiv Grav (GN01) gripper,
always pointing straight down; yaw turns it about the vertical. tcp is the
fingertip point between the fingers (site gn01_grasp_center_site in the model),
0.198 m from the flange. Its model files are in robot_real/ (rizon4s_gn01.xml;
frames and wrench conventions in robot_real/README.md). robot/ holds the
simulated floating-stage robot of the sim exam: do not use it for this cell.
The arm itself, not only the gripper, can touch objects: keep that in mind when
you plan and when you model contacts. Some targets are out of reach or rejected
by the safety layer. Robot, controller and sensors are fixed; only the scene is
unknown.

Submission
--------------
Submit one folder for your seed, submission/<seed>/:
    twin/            the twin in your engine(s)' format: objects, table, room,
                     lights and materials (visual and collision geometry, mass,
                     inertia, friction, joints), object poses at the canonical
                     start state, and the robot from robot_real/ with your
                     replay of its controller. Meshes and textures by relative
                     path.
    predict/         a runnable predictor with its environment, a README and a
                     smoke test (interface below). It must not call any language
                     or vision model or network service, and apart from the
                     request and output paths it may read only files in its own
                     submission folder.
    params.json      every estimated quantity per object
    claims.json      every claim about your twin, with the path to its evidence
    probe_log.jsonl  the probe log (format below)
    agent_audit.md   and report.md, as in Section 22

params.json is a list of entries such as
  {"object": "obj_0", "name": "friction_slide", "value": 0.42,
   "unit": "1", "uncertainty": {"type": "interval", "low": 0.35, "high": 0.50},
   "status": "inferred", "evidence": "fits/fit_003.json"}
where status is exactly one of "observed", "inferred", "assumed".
claims.json is a list of entries such as
  {"claim": "object mass is 0.31 kg", "value": 0.31, "unit": "kg",
   "status": "inferred", "evidence": "fits/fit_002.json"}
probe_log.jsonl: one JSON object per line, two per probe with the same probe_id,
appended and never edited. Before the action:
  {"probe_id": "p001", "phase": "plan", "time": "<ISO 8601>",
   "beliefs": "...", "uncertain": ["..."],
   "candidates": [{"action": {"primitive": "push", "params": {}},
                   "expected_reveal": "...", "rejected_because": null}],
   "chosen": 0, "reason": "...", "decision_source": "judgement|script|both"}
After the result:
  {"probe_id": "p001", "phase": "result", "time": "<ISO 8601>",
   "request_file": "requests/0007.json", "status": "ok", "learned": "..."}

Predictor interface. The evaluator runs, from submission/<seed>/predict/:
  python predict.py --request request.json --out <dir>
request.json:
  {"episodes": [{"actions": [{"primitive": "...", "params": {...}}, ...]}, ...],
   "views":    [{"name": "...", "width": W, "height": H, "K": [[3x3]],
                 "T_world_cam": [[4x4]]}, ...],
   "dt": <sample period in s, the logging rate from /v1/info>}
Every episode starts from the canonical start state (the scene as you first
observed it) and runs its actions in order, in exactly the format of
/v1/execute. Cameras use the OpenCV convention; T_world_cam maps camera to world
coordinates. A view without "after" is rendered at the canonical start state; a
view with "after": {"episode": e, "action": k} is rendered at the state after
that action, once the objects have come to rest.
Write to <dir>:
  episodes/<e>/action_<k>.npz  (e, k zero-based) with arrays t [T], tcp [T,3],
      yaw [T], width [T], wrench [T,6] (tool frame at the flange, as the API
      reports it), obj_pos [T,N,3], obj_quat [T,N,4] (w,x,y,z, world frame),
      sampled every dt from the start of the action, and a JSON string "meta"
      with status ("ok", "protective_stop" or "error" with a message) and
      events, named as in the API.
  views/<name>.npz with rgb uint8 [H,W,3] and depth float32 [H,W] in metres
      (0 = invalid).
  objects.json: the object names in the order of the N axis.
The predictor runs without network access, within 10 minutes per call; say in
predict/README whether it needs a GPU and fall back to CPU if it can. If an
action fails in your twin, write what you have with status "error"; do not
crash the call. Leave the folder under PROJECT_ROOT; the operator collects it. A
twin is final once written there and marked submitted in status.json. After
your first probe, keep a crude but runnable fallback twin there (passing its
smoke test), overwrite it with each accepted improvement, and mark claims
"draft": true until the explicit final submission.


20. Records
-----------
Inside PROJECT_ROOT:
  requests/     every request you sent and every response you received
  probe_log.jsonl
  beliefs/      hypotheses, estimates and uncertainty after each update
  twins/        each twin version, with what changed and why
  fits/         fitting inputs, losses, gradients and diagnostics
  compare/      source versus twin images and replayed trajectories
  submission/   the final twin
And for the run: run.json (start time, code and environment hashes, compute
used) and report.md.

When saving requests, remove the Authorization header (or replace it with
"Bearer ***"); save the method, path, parameters and body as usual. Never write
the token into code, configuration files, logs, commits, reports or
submissions; read it from an environment variable at run time.

Also:
  status.json       in_progress / passed / failed / blocked + actual last step;
  attempts/         every failure, stack trace, scene dump, short repro.
Avoid external auto-writing caches; configure TMPDIR, XDG_CACHE_HOME and
package/model caches inside PROJECT_ROOT. Absolute tool output paths must
resolve under PROJECT_ROOT, otherwise reject the write and fix the tool
configuration before running. No overwrite of old results. Do not spend time
rendering lengthy videos before your twin predicts well.

     (type, pose, colour, intensity, uncertainty, evidence) and per-surface

     separately from geometry residuals.
  5. What failed, causal diagnostics, controlled changes and remaining unknowns.
  6. What you could not determine, and why.
  7. MP4 videos comparing recorded source-world observations (left) with
     synchronized twin renders (right), from the available cameras, for every
     executed action. Save them under PROJECT_ROOT/outputs/videos/; report any
     comparison you could not make.
  agent_audit.md: per meaningful action (probes and camera moves), what was
     done, why it beat the alternatives, its expected and actual information

     actually used, with evidence; strategy, failures and human interventions.
     Base it on the rationale logged before each action, not on hindsight.
Keep normal progress updates brief; continue implementation rather than asking
for permission at every minor simulated adjustment within the approved task.
Report precisely what you could and could not infer; an honest partial result
is worth more than a confident wrong one.
