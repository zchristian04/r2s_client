Agent Handoff: Agentic Real-to-Sim for Robotic Manipulation
===========================================================

Core task:
"Reconstruct everything in the observed scene into a high-fidelity digital twin.
The simulated images should match the source images as closely as possible, and
the simulated robot interactions must predict new physical outcomes. You must
independently inspect, experiment, correct, render, compare, and repeat."
The human specifies the goal, available tools, resource/safety constraints and
scientific validation — not the sequence of actions. You, the Agent, must
figure out whether and how to look from another viewpoint, touch, push, grasp,
lift, turn, relocate an occluder, fit geometry, identify friction or mass, and
correct material or lighting. Do not ask the user which specific motion to
perform or require object-specific rules. A tool library is not an action plan.
The instruction "exactly the same" is the rigorous optimization target, not
permission to invent a perfect metric, hidden back surfaces or unknown physics.
Both rendered-image errors and predictive-contact errors must be measured.
A correct mass with incorrect shape, handle, colors or light is not a finished
scene; a pretty render with incorrect physical contacts is not a finished twin.
Preserve negative evidence and report uncertainty rather than faking a 100%
score. 

You are the implementing research Agent. Do the work; do not merely write a plan.
Choose the best verified simulator(s) for your
twin, including MuJoCo/MJX, Isaac Sim, NVIDIA Newton or other suitable engines.
Reconstruct and compare objects, support, background, basic colors, materials, shadows and illumination — do not
defer visual verification. High-fidelity renderers and more expressive lighting
can be added as image residuals show the need. Conversely, do not invent success, cheat by reading held-out
ground truth, or silently relax metrics.


4. Project Summary (for Orientation Only)
-----------------------------------------
Goal: From calibrated RGB-D sensing, a known robot model and bounded robot-control
API, reconstruct an initially unfamiliar workspace into a digital twin that can
predict robot-object contact outcomes. Here the source world is the real scene;
its ground truth (object dimensions, masses, poses, friction) is measured by the
operators and kept hidden from you.

Your job is to turn what you observe and what you learn by
interacting into a digital twin of that scene.

A good twin does two things:
  - its rendered images match the camera images (geometry, colors, materials,
    table, background, lights and shadows), including scene states you never saw;
  - it predicts new physical outcomes: when robot actions you never executed are
    run on the real scene, the objects in your twin move the way the real ones do.

This run uses a real robot. Every motion you request is checked by a safety
layer and runs right away; a human operator watches the arm and holds the
emergency stop. Real
sensors are noisy, real objects are not perfect primitives, and nothing you do
can be undone: there are no resets (Section 5).

Scientific loop: hypothesize -> choose informative probe -> execute -> observe ->
update uncertainty -> locally refine -> validate on new actions -> repeat.

The target is not only appearance. Visual geometry, collision geometry, joints,
physical properties, poses, materials and lighting must remain consistent inside
one canonical scene model. Physical prediction on genuinely new manipulations is
the primary outcome.

Division of labor:
  Agent: choose structural hypotheses, unknown regions and safe probes; schedule
  tools and numerical refinement; interpret residuals; accept/reject versions;
  in multi-object scenes decide when to directly probe a target versus first
  clearing an occlusion, using another available camera, or moving a neighbor.
  Numerical solvers: fit mass, friction, supported contact shape, pose, inertia,
  joint parameters and appearance/lighting from observations.
  Robot controller (server side): execute bounded primitives, with independent
  safety guards.
  Evaluator (examiner side): assess frozen predictions against hidden truth, not
  supply hints.

 Everything in a twin render must be simulated: the objects, the robot, the table, the walls, the floor, the background and the lighting. Do not use camera images, crops of them or anything derived pixel by pixel from them as a backdrop, background plate, environment map, texture, mask fill
  or composite layer, and do not paste or blend observed pixels into a
  render. Model the background as 3D geometry with fitted colours and materials,
  and lit by your fitted lights; observed images may only be used as targets to
  compare against and to fit parameters. A render that contains observed pixels does
  not count asa reconstruction, and any image-match metric computed on it is
  invalid.


5. The API
----------
The server URL and your token are given separately. Every request carries the
header "Authorization: Bearer <token>". JSON requests; array responses come as
NumPy .npz files (load with allow_pickle=False) with a JSON string "meta".
Units are SI (metres, seconds, radians, newtons). World frame: z up, the table
top is the plane z = 0, origin directly below the robot base; x points away from
the robot towards the camera, y to the robot's left. The robot base sits on the
table at (0, 0, 0.0134).

  GET  /v1/info          description, your scene, budgets, cameras,
                         primitives, logging rate, table extent
  GET  /v1/budget        remaining budget
The API calls your scene a "seed"; you have exactly one, listed by /v1/info.

  POST /v1/reset         {"seed": "<your scene>"}  call once, to start; it sends
                         the robot to its home pose and waits for the operator
  GET  /v1/calibration   per camera: width, height, K (OpenCV convention),
                         T_parent_cam, parent frame; depth in metres along the
                         optical axis, 0 = invalid
  POST /v1/observe       {"camera": "zed"}
                         returns rgb, depth, t, tcp_meas, yaw_meas
  POST /v1/execute       {"primitive": "...", "params": {...}}
                         returns t, tcp_cmd, tcp_meas, yaw_cmd, yaw_meas,
                         width_cmd, width_meas, wrench, frame_t, frames_rgb,
                         frames_depth; meta has status ("ok",
                         "protective_stop" or "rejected", with a reason) and
                         events (stop_force, protective_stop, target_not_reached)

There is one camera, "zed": a fixed ZED 2i stereo RGB-D camera, 1280 x 720, in
front of the robot looking back at it. It never moves. Its depth is computed
from stereo, not measured directly.

One episode, no resets. The scene as you first see it is the canonical start
state. Every action changes the real scene, and nobody puts it back: the scene
you see after an action is the scene your next action acts on. Re-observe after
every action.

 Actions take tens of seconds: use request timeouts of 10 minutes or
  more.
  A protective stop ends the action early, backs off 2 cm, is free, 
  and you
  can continue after re-observing. HTTP 409 means the operator must 
  clear a
  fault: retry about once a minute. "target_not_reached" means the 
  arm stopped
  short; always use tcp_meas, never the commanded target. Force is 
  estimated
  from joint torques: about 1 N of noise.

Ensure that there is no bias in perception before performing any actions.

Primitives (the robot is the full arm; you command its gripper):
  move_to     {tcp: [x,y,z], yaw?, speed?}                         free move
  home        {}                                                   free move
  push        {contact_point: [x,y,z], direction: [dx,dy],
               distance, speed, yaw?}                              contact
  touch       {point: [x,y,z], direction: [dx,dy,dz], max_travel,
               speed, stop_force, yaw?}                            contact
  grasp_lift  {center: [x,y,z], yaw, open_width, lift, hold?}      contact

Each primitive rises to a safe travel height, moves above its start point, does
its work and returns to the travel height. A touch stops when the measured force
exceeds stop_force. Any action stops if the force exceeds the protective limit.
Parameter ranges are enforced by the server; an invalid call is rejected with a
message and costs nothing.

The robot is a Flexiv Rizon 4s 7-axis arm with a Flexiv Grav (GN01) gripper,
always pointing straight down; yaw turns it about the vertical. tcp is the
fingertip point between the fingers (site gn01_grasp_center_site in the model),
0.198 m from the flange. Its model files are in robot_real/ (rizon4s_gn01.xml;
frames and wrench conventions in robot_real/README.md). robot/ holds the
simulated floating-stage robot of the sim exam: do not use it for this cell.
The arm itself, not only
the gripper, can touch objects: keep that in mind when you plan and when you
model contacts. Some targets are out of reach or rejected by the safety layer.

17. Submission
--------------
Submit one twin per seed, as one folder per seed:
  <seed>/
    twin/            the twin in whatever format your chosen engine(s) use
                     (for example MJCF, USD, a Newton scene or Blender files):
                     objects, table, lights and materials (visual and collision
                     geometry, mass, inertia, friction), object poses at the
                     canonical episode-0 state, and the robot from robot/ with
                     your replay of its controller. Include meshes and textures
                     by relative path.
    predict/         a runnable predictor for your twin, with its environment
                     (requirements or container), a README saying how to set it
                     up, and a smoke test. Interface below. Predictor rules: it
                     must not call any language or vision model or any network
                     service, and apart from the request and output paths it is
                     given, it may read only files inside its own submission
                     folder.
    params.json      every estimated quantity per object: value, uncertainty
                     (standard deviation or interval) and whether it was
                     observed, inferred or assumed
    probe_log.jsonl  the log from Section 10
    claims.json      every claim you make about your twin, one entry each:
                     what is claimed, the value, and the path to the evidence
    report.md        the report described in Section 22

File formats (one JSON object per entry; field names are required):

params.json is a list of entries such as
  {"object": "obj_0", "name": "friction_slide", "value": 0.42,
   "unit": "1", "uncertainty": {"type": "interval", "low": 0.35, "high": 0.50},
   "status": "inferred", "evidence": "fits/fit_003.json"}
where status is exactly one of "observed", "inferred", "assumed".

claims.json is a list of entries such as
  {"claim": "object mass is 0.31 kg", "value": 0.31, "unit": "kg",
   "status": "inferred", "evidence": "fits/fit_002.json"}

probe_log.jsonl has one JSON object per line. Each probe writes two lines with
the same probe_id, appended and never edited. Before the action:
  {"probe_id": "p001", "phase": "plan", "time": "<ISO 8601>",
   "beliefs": "...", "uncertain": ["..."],
   "candidates": [{"action": {"primitive": "push", "params": {}},
                   "expected_reveal": "...", "rejected_because": null}],
   "chosen": 0, "reason": "...", "decision_source": "judgement|script|both"}
After the result:
  {"probe_id": "p001", "phase": "result", "time": "<ISO 8601>",
   "request_file": "requests/0007.json", "status": "ok",
   "learned": "..."}

Predictor interface. The evaluator runs, from submission/<seed>/predict/:
  python predict.py --request request.json --out <dir>
request.json:
  {"episodes": [{"actions": [{"primitive": "...", "params": {...}}, ...]}, ...],
   "views":    [{"name": "...", "width": W, "height": H, "K": [[3x3]],
                 "T_world_cam": [[4x4]]}, ...],
   "dt": <sample period in s, the logging rate from /v1/info>}
  Every episode starts from the canonical start state (the scene as you first
  observed it) and runs its actions in order (an episode may hold one action or
  several). Actions use exactly the format of /v1/execute. Cameras use the
  OpenCV convention, as in /v1/calibration; T_world_cam maps camera to world
  coordinates. A view without "after" is rendered at the canonical start state;
  a view with "after": {"episode": e, "action": k} is rendered at the state
  after that action, once the objects have come to rest.
Write to <dir>:
  episodes/<e>/action_<k>.npz  (e, k zero-based) with arrays
      t [T], tcp [T,3], yaw [T], width [T], wrench [T,6] (tool frame at the
      flange, [Fx Fy Fz Tx Ty Tz], as the API reports it), obj_pos [T,N,3],
      obj_quat [T,N,4] (w,x,y,z, world frame), sampled every dt from the start
      of the action, and a JSON string "meta" with status ("ok" or
      "protective_stop") and events, named as in the API.
  views/<name>.npz with rgb uint8 [H,W,3] and depth float32 [H,W] in metres
      (0 = invalid).
  objects.json: the object names in the order of the N axis.
The predictor runs without network access, within 10 minutes per call. A GPU
may be present but is not guaranteed; say in predict/README whether yours needs
one and fall back to CPU if it can. If an action fails in your twin, write what
you have and set status to "error" with a message; do not crash the whole call.

Use the API's conventions: world z up, table top at z = 0, SI units. You choose
the engine and format; the predictor is what is graded.
For this run, leave each seed's folder in submission/<seed>/ under PROJECT_ROOT;
the operator collects it after the run. A twin is final once you write it there
and mark the seed as submitted in status.json.


20. Records
-----------
Inside PROJECT_ROOT, per seed:
  requests/     every request you sent and every response you received
  probe_log.jsonl
  beliefs/      hypotheses, estimates and uncertainty after each update
  twins/        each twin version you built, with what changed and why
  fits/         fitting inputs, losses, gradients and diagnostics
  compare/      source versus twin images and replayed trajectories
  submission/   the final twin
And for the run: run.json (start time, code and environment hashes, compute
used) and report.md.

When saving requests, remove the Authorization header (or replace it with
"Bearer ***"); save the method, path, parameters and body as usual. Never write
the token into code, configuration files, logs, commits, reports or
submissions; read it from an environment variable (for example R2S_TOKEN) at
run time.

Also, from v2.4:
  status.json       in_progress / passed / failed / blocked + actual last step;
  attempts/         every failure, stack trace, scene dump, short repro.
Avoid external auto-writing caches; configure TMPDIR, XDG_CACHE_HOME and
package/model caches inside PROJECT_ROOT. Absolute tool output paths must
resolve under PROJECT_ROOT, otherwise reject the write and fix the tool
configuration before running. No overwrite of old results. Do not spend time
rendering lengthy marketing videos before your twin predicts well.
