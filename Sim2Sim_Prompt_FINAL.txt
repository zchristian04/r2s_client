Agent Handoff: Agentic Sim-to-Sim for Robotic Manipulation
==========================================================

Core task:
"Reconstruct everything in the observed scene into a high-fidelity digital twin.
The simulated images should match the source images as closely as possible, and
the simulated robot interactions must predict new physical outcomes. You must
independently inspect, experiment, correct, render, compare, and repeat."

You are the implementing research Agent.

"Exactly the same" is the optimization target, not permission to invent a perfect
metric, hidden back surfaces or unknown physics. Measure both rendered-image
errors and predictive-contact errors. A correct mass with incorrect shape, handle,
colors or light is not a finished scene; a pretty render with incorrect contacts
is not a finished twin. Preserve negative evidence and report uncertainty rather
than faking a 100% score. Do not invent success, read held-out ground truth, or
silently relax metrics. Do not stop merely because a script ran, an image looks
good or a loss decreased.

Choose the best verified simulator(s) for your twin.
  - possible physics engines: PhysX (via Isaac Sim), MuJoCo/MJX, Newton
  - possible renderers: Blender/Cycles, Isaac Sim RTX

Reconstruct and compare objects, support, background, colors, materials, shadows
and illumination from the start; do not defer visual verification. Add
higher-fidelity renderers and richer lighting as image residuals show the need.
You may use separate engines for physics and rendering, provided both read the
same canonical scene and every rendered frame is driven by simulated state; the
evaluator must be able to run your full pipeline from your submission. You may need to ask for GPU access. If something you need is not installed,
ask. Choose the engines that give the most faithful recreation, not the easiest.


Project summary (orientation only)
----------------------------------
A hidden scene runs in a separate simulated "source world" that you reach only
through a robot and sensor API over the network, like a real lab rig. There is
no real robot. From calibrated RGB-D sensing, a known robot model and the API,
reconstruct the scene for your seed into a twin. Ground truth (shapes, masses,
poses, friction, joints, lights, materials) is hidden.

A good twin:
  - renders images that match the cameras (geometry, colors, materials, table,
    room, lights, shadows), including views and scene states you never saw;
  - predicts new physical outcomes: when robot actions you never executed are
    run, your twin's objects move the way the source world's objects do.

Lighting and texture are as important as geometry. Model the lights (number,
type, position, direction, colour, intensity) from shading, cast shadows and
highlights, and surfaces as textures where they show patterns, with shine
estimated from highlights. Never bake lighting into textures: compare images
before and after an object moves and across cameras to separate light from
material. Camera exposure and white balance are fixed; do not fit them per
image. Mark surfaces you never observed as unobserved.

You should try using algorithms to figure out what the physical and appearance
parameters are. Examples include (but are by no means limited to) system ID,
inverse rendering, real2sim, etc.

Rendering rule
--------------
Everything in a twin render must be simulated: objects, robot, table, walls,
floor, background and lighting. Model the background as 3D geometry with fitted
colors and materials, lit by your fitted lights. Observed images may only be
used as comparison targets and for fitting parameters. Never use camera images,
crops of them, or anything derived pixel by pixel from them as a backdrop,
background plate, environment map, texture, mask fill or composite layer, and
never paste or blend observed pixels into a render.

No photo textures: every material must be procedural (shader nodes, built-in
textures, flat colors) or come from a standard asset library, with its
parameters tuned to match the images. Sampling colors or roughness values from
the images is allowed; textures cropped, projected, or generated from them
(including by image-conditioned generative models) are not. A render that
contains observed pixels does not count as a reconstruction, and any
image-match metric computed on it is invalid.


The API
----------
The server URL and your token are given separately (S2S_SERVER_URL, S2S_TOKEN).
Every request carries the header "Authorization: Bearer <token>". JSON requests;
array responses come as NumPy .npz files (load with allow_pickle=False) with a
JSON string "meta". Units are SI (metres, seconds, radians, newtons). World
frame: z up, the table top is the plane z = 0.

  GET  /v1/info          description, your seed, budgets, cameras, primitives,
                         logging rate, table extent, TCP workspace
  GET  /v1/budget        remaining budget
  POST /v1/reset         {"seed": "<your seed>"}  start an episode (a job)
  GET  /v1/calibration   per camera: width, height, K (OpenCV convention),
                         T_parent_cam, parent frame; depth in metres along the
                         optical axis, 0 = invalid
  POST /v1/observe       {"camera": "cam_0" | "wrist"}  returns rgb, depth, t;
                         the wrist camera also tcp_meas [3] and rpy_meas [3]
  POST /v1/execute       {"primitive": "...", "params": {...}}  (a job)
                         returns t, tcp_cmd, tcp_meas [N,3], rpy_cmd, rpy_meas
                         [N,3], width_cmd, width_meas [N], wrench [N,6],
                         frame_t, frames_rgb, frames_depth (cam_0, reduced
                         resolution); meta has status ("ok" or
                         "protective_stop"), events (stop_force,
                         protective_stop) and the remaining budget
  GET  /v1/jobs/<id>?wait=<s>   the result of a job

Jobs: actions render their frames as they run, so a contact action often takes
1-3 minutes and a free move tens of seconds. POST /v1/reset and /v1/execute
answer at once with HTTP 202 {"job": "<id>"}; GET /v1/jobs/<id>?wait=50 (at
most 60) answers 202 while it runs, then exactly what the call returns. Fetching
again never charges again. While a job runs, other world calls answer 409.
Observations take 0.5-2 s and answer directly. client/s2s_client.py (numpy and
requests only) does this for you: reset() and execute() block and return.
Errors: 400 invalid call (costs nothing), 401 token, 403 not your seed, 409 job
running or no episode yet, 429 budget exhausted, 500 source world failed (call
reset; the episode is lost, charges stay), 503 busy.

Episodes: episode 0 is the canonical start state. Each reset starts a new
episode with every free object moved by a small, human-like error (a few
millimetres, under a degree); furniture stays put. The scene after an action is
the scene your next action acts on; re-observe after actions. The wrist camera
is on the tool; its extrinsics are relative to the tool flange.

Motion: you design every motion yourself; there is no menu of actions. The robot
is a floating gripper on a stage with x, y, z, roll, pitch and yaw, and its
general command is a trajectory: waypoints your own code computes. Pushing,
pulling, grasping from above or the side, lifting, tilting, regrasping, opening
a door or drawer and clearing an obstruction are all trajectories.

Orientation "rpy": [roll, pitch, yaw] in rad, R = Rz(yaw) Ry(pitch) Rx(roll),
|roll|, |pitch| <= pi/2, yaw in [-pi, pi] (no wrapping). rpy = 0 points the
fingers straight down, opening along world ±y. "yaw": a is shorthand for
rpy [0, 0, a]; give one or the other. Rotations turn about the TCP, each axis on
its own smooth profile at up to 1.5 rad/s.

  trajectory  {waypoints: [{tcp: [x,y,z], rpy? | yaw?, width?, speed?, hold?,
               stop_force?, stiffness?, rot_stiffness?}, ...]}   1-50 waypoints
    tcp            fingertip point to reach (TCP; see robot/)
    width          jaw opening, 0 (closed) to 0.100 m
    speed          TCP speed, 0.002-0.10 m/s (default 0.10)
    hold           seconds to stay once reached (0-2)
    stop_force     N (0.5-30): stop where it is and skip the remaining
                   waypoints when the measured force exceeds it; such a
                   waypoint may aim slightly below the table, otherwise tcp z
                   must be >= 0.002
    stiffness      N/m, one number or [kx, ky, kz] (world), 50-4000 (default
                   4000, stiff)
    rot_stiffness  N m/rad, one number or [roll, pitch, yaw], 0.2-60 (default
                   60, stiff)
  rpy, width, stiffness and rot_stiffness carry over until you change them.

Shortcuts rise to the travel height (TCP z = 0.38 m), move above their start
point, do their work and rise again:
  move_to     {tcp: [x,y,z], rpy? | yaw?, speed?}
  home        {}
  push        {contact_point: [x,y,z], direction: [dx,dy], distance, speed,
               rpy? | yaw?}        (default: facing the push direction)
  touch       {point: [x,y,z], direction: [dx,dy,dz], max_travel, speed,
               stop_force, rpy? | yaw?}
  grasp_lift  {center: [x,y,z], rpy | yaw, open_width, lift, hold?}: comes in
              6 cm along the fingers, closes, waits for them to settle, lifts,
              holds, sets down until contact, opens and withdraws

Budget: a trajectory is charged after it runs, as a contact action if the
gripper touched anything (including the table), else as a free move; it needs
one contact action left to start. Shortcuts are charged by type (/v1/info lists
contact_primitives). The first reset is free. Ranges are enforced by the
server; an invalid call is rejected with a message and costs nothing.

The robot is a Flexiv Grav (GN01) gripper on a floating stage with roll, pitch
and yaw; its model files are in robot/. Robot, controller and sensors are fixed;
only the scene is unknown.

Submission
--------------
Submit one folder for your seed, submission/<seed>/:
    twin/            the twin in your engine(s)' format: objects, table, room,
                     lights and materials (visual and collision geometry, mass,
                     inertia, friction, joints), object poses at the canonical
                     episode-0 state, and the robot from robot/ with your replay
                     of its controller. Meshes and textures by relative path.
    predict/         a runnable predictor with its environment, a README and a
                     smoke test (interface below). It must not call any language
                     or vision model or network service, and apart from the
                     request and output paths it may read only files in its own
                     submission folder.
    params.json      every estimated quantity per object
    claims.json      every claim about your twin, with the path to its evidence
    probe_log.jsonl  the log from Section 7
    agent_audit.md   and report.md, as in Section 22

params.json is a list of entries such as
  {"object": "obj_0", "name": "friction_slide", "value": 0.42,
   "unit": "1", "uncertainty": {"type": "interval", "low": 0.35, "high": 0.50},
   "status": "inferred", "evidence": "fits/fit_003.json"}
where status is exactly one of "observed", "inferred", "assumed".
claims.json is a list of entries such as
  {"claim": "object mass is 0.31 kg", "value": 0.31, "unit": "kg",
   "status": "inferred", "evidence": "fits/fit_002.json"}
probe_log.jsonl: one JSON object per line, two per probe with the same probe_id,
appended and never edited. Before the action:
  {"probe_id": "p001", "phase": "plan", "time": "<ISO 8601>",
   "beliefs": "...", "uncertain": ["..."],
   "candidates": [{"action": {"primitive": "push", "params": {}},
                   "expected_reveal": "...", "rejected_because": null}],
   "chosen": 0, "reason": "...", "decision_source": "judgement|script|both"}
After the result:
  {"probe_id": "p001", "phase": "result", "time": "<ISO 8601>",
   "request_file": "requests/0007.json", "status": "ok", "learned": "..."}

Predictor interface. The evaluator runs, from submission/<seed>/predict/:
  python predict.py --request request.json --out <dir>
request.json:
  {"episodes": [{"actions": [{"primitive": "...", "params": {...}}, ...]}, ...],
   "views":    [{"name": "...", "width": W, "height": H, "K": [[3x3]],
                 "T_world_cam": [[4x4]]}, ...],
   "dt": <sample period in s, the logging rate from /v1/info>}
Every episode starts from the canonical episode-0 state and runs its actions in
order, in exactly the format of /v1/execute. Cameras use the OpenCV convention;
T_world_cam maps camera to world coordinates. Views are rendered at the
canonical episode-0 state.
Write to <dir>:
  episodes/<e>/action_<k>.npz  (e, k zero-based) with arrays t [T], tcp [T,3],
      rpy [T,3], width [T], wrench [T,6] (tool frame at the flange, as the API
      reports it), obj_pos [T,N,3], obj_quat [T,N,4] (w,x,y,z, world frame),
      sampled every dt from the start of the action, and a JSON string "meta"
      with status ("ok", "protective_stop" or "error" with a message) and
      events, named as in the API.
  views/<name>.npz with rgb uint8 [H,W,3] and depth float32 [H,W] in metres
      (0 = invalid).
  objects.json: the object names in the order of the N axis.
The predictor runs without network access, within 10 minutes per call; say in
predict/README whether it needs a GPU and fall back to CPU if it can. If an
action fails in your twin, write what you have with status "error"; do not
crash the call. Leave the folder under PROJECT_ROOT; the operator collects it. A
twin is final once written there and marked submitted in status.json. After
your first probe, keep a crude but runnable fallback twin there (passing its
smoke test), overwrite it with each accepted improvement, and mark claims
"draft": true until the explicit final submission.


20. Records
-----------
Inside PROJECT_ROOT:
  requests/     every request you sent and every response you received
  probe_log.jsonl
  beliefs/      hypotheses, estimates and uncertainty after each update
  twins/        each twin version, with what changed and why
  fits/         fitting inputs, losses, gradients and diagnostics
  compare/      source versus twin images and replayed trajectories
  submission/   the final twin
And for the run: run.json (start time, code and environment hashes, compute
used) and report.md.

When saving requests, remove the Authorization header (or replace it with
"Bearer ***"); save the method, path, parameters and body as usual. Never write
the token into code, configuration files, logs, commits, reports or
submissions; read it from S2S_TOKEN at run time.

Also:
  status.json       in_progress / passed / failed / blocked + actual last step;
  attempts/         every failure, stack trace, scene dump, short repro.
Avoid external auto-writing caches; configure TMPDIR, XDG_CACHE_HOME and
package/model caches inside PROJECT_ROOT. Absolute tool output paths must
resolve under PROJECT_ROOT, otherwise reject the write and fix the tool
configuration before running. No overwrite of old results. Do not spend time
rendering lengthy videos before your twin predicts well.

  6. What you could not determine, and why.

     Base it on the rationale logged before each action, not on hindsight.
Keep normal progress updates brief; continue implementation rather than asking
for permission at every minor simulated adjustment within the approved task.
Report precisely what you could and could not infer; an honest partial result
is worth more than a confident wrong one.
